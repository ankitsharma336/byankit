1. Beams: Design Mechanics & Theory

Beam design under IS 456 follows the Limit State Method (LSM). Flexural strain distribution is assumed parabolic-rectangular with a maximum concrete compressive strain of 0.0035 at the extreme fiber. Steel yields at 0.87fy/Es+0.002.

Step 1: Effective Span (leff) — Cl. 22.2

Smaller of lclear+d or lclear+w.

Step 2: Factored Load Actions — Cl. 36.4 & Table 18

Include self-weight wsw=b·D·25 kN/m3. Total factored load wu=1.5(wsw+DL+LL). Moment Mu=wuleff2/8.

Step 3: Flexure Capacity & Tension Steel — Cl. 38.1 & Annex G

Mu,lim = 0.36· fck· xu,lim d ·bd2 · (1-0.42 xu,limd )

If Mu⩽Mu,lim, calculate tension steel Ast via Annex G quadratic equation.

Step 4: Shear Design Mechanics — Cl. 40

Nominal shear τv=Vub·d. Concrete shear strength τc interpolated from Table 19 based on steel percentage pt. Design stirrups for excess shear Vus=Vu-τcbd.

Step 5: Serviceability (Deflection) — Cl. 23.2.1

Check (l/d)act⩽20·Kt, where Kt depends on service stress fs=0.58fy·Ast,reqAst,prov.

2. One-Way Slabs: Theory & Mechanics

A slab is classified as One-Way when the long-to-short span ratio ly/lx>2.0. Loads transfer predominantly along the shorter span direction. One-way slabs are analyzed as a series of 1-meter wide (b=1000 mm) rectangular flexural strips.

Step 1: Effective Depth & Dead Load

Assume depth d≈l/25 to l/30. Self-weight wsw=1.0·D·25 kN/m2. Total factored load wu=1.5(wsw+DL+LL) kN/m.

Step 2: Flexural Design (Main Steel Ast) — Cl. 38.1

Calculate main reinforcement per meter width (b=1000 mm) using Annex G formula. Spacing of main bars s=1000·Abar/Ast. Maximum main bar spacing s⩽min(3d,300 mm) (Cl. 26.3.3).

Step 3: Minimum & Distribution Steel — Cl. 26.5.2.1

Provide temperature/shrinkage steel perpendicular to main steel:

  • Fe 415 / Fe 500: Ast,dist=0.0012·b·D=0.12%bD
  • Mild Steel Fe 250: Ast,dist=0.15%bD
  • Maximum distribution bar spacing s⩽min(5d,450 mm).

Step 4: Shear Strength Check — Cl. 40.2.1

Solid slabs possess enhanced shear capacity. Permissible shear stress τc=k·τc,table19, where k ranges from 1.3 (for D⩽150 mm) to 1.0. Shear stirrups are generally avoided in slabs by ensuring τv⩽kτc.

3. Two-Way Slabs: Theory & Mechanics

Slabs with aspect ratio ly/lx⩽2.0 bend in both orthogonal directions. Bending moments depend on whether corners are free to lift (Simply Supported - Marcus/Rankine-Grashof method) or restrained against lifting (Annex D coefficients).

Step 1: Moment Calculation

Design bending moments along short (lx) and long (ly) spans:

Mux=αx·wu·lx2

Muy=αy·wu·lx2

Step 2: Reinforcement Design along Both Directions

Main short-span steel Ast,x is placed at bottom outer layer (effective depth dx). Long-span steel Ast,y rests on top of short-span steel (effective depth dy=dx-ϕx+ϕy2).

Step 3: Corner Reinforcement — Annex D

Corner reinforcement depends on the edge condition. For two discontinuous meeting edges, Annex D-1.8 requires 0.75 of the maximum midspan steel in each of four layers over 0.2 times the shorter span. For Table 26 Case 1 (all four edges continuous), no corner torsion reinforcement is required under D-1.10.

Atorsion = 0.75·Ast,x,max  per layer, over  0.2·lx
