Detailed Septic Tank Design Procedure

Use this workflow for an Indian household installation. The app separates the hydraulic, storage, dimensional, construction and disposal decisions so that each can be checked independently.

Step 1 — Establish the design population

Determine the number of persons expected to use the system. For a house, use the design occupancy rather than only the present occupants where future occupancy is reasonably foreseeable.

Input: Population, P persons.

Step 2 — Establish wastewater flow

Determine the actual wastewater contribution. If a reliable measured value is unavailable, use the applicable local design water-supply/wastewater criterion and clearly state the assumption.

Calculation: Q = P × q, where q is wastewater generation in L/person/day.

Important: The standard household dimensions should not be blindly combined with an assumed flow if kitchen, bath and other wastewater are connected. Confirm the scope of the drainage system.

Step 3 — Select detention time

Adopt the applicable detention period for septic-tank treatment. This app permits 24–48 h as the design range represented in IS 2470.

Hydraulic volume: Vd = Q × t / 24, with Q in m³/day and t in hours.

Step 4 — Determine sludge-storage requirement

Allow for accumulation between desludging operations. The app uses the IS 2470 design accumulation value of approximately 0.00021 m³/person/day.

Sludge volume: Vs = 0.00021 × P × N × 365, where N is the desludging interval in years.

Do not assume that hydraulic capacity alone is sufficient. The tank must have adequate storage and must be emptied at an appropriate interval.

Step 5 — Select tank dimensions

For household systems, first consult the applicable IS 2470 recommended dimension table. Then verify the general dimensional requirements.

  • Minimum width: 0.75 m.
  • Minimum liquid depth: 1.0 m.
  • Minimum liquid capacity: 1.0 m³.
  • Rectangular length/width ratio: 2 to 4.
  • Minimum freeboard: 0.30 m.

For larger populations, use a detailed multi-compartment design rather than simply scaling a small household tank indefinitely.

Step 6 — Check settling surface area

Check the tank plan area against the peak-flow relationship represented by IS 2470:

A = 0.92 × Qp / 10

where A is surface area in m² and Qp is peak flow in L/min.

The provided surface area should satisfy the applicable requirement. Peak flow should preferably come from a proper plumbing/fixture-flow assessment rather than an arbitrary value.

Step 7 — Decide number of compartments

Use the applicable IS 2470 configuration. A two-compartment arrangement is used for larger tanks and provides better separation of primary settling/sludge functions from the outlet compartment.

Provide access to each compartment for inspection and desludging. The internal partition, inlet and outlet arrangements must prevent short-circuiting and disturbance of settled solids.

Step 8 — Design inlet and outlet

  • Provide a suitable submerged/T arrangement at the inlet to reduce disturbance.
  • For tanks below 1200 mm width, the app represents the 100 mm nominal bore outlet dip-pipe arrangement.
  • Keep the outlet invert approximately 50 mm below the inlet invert where the applicable IS 2470 detail applies.
  • Provide sufficient submergence and prevent floating scum from escaping.

Step 9 — Provide ventilation and access

Provide ventilation through a suitable vent pipe. The app uses the IS 2470 minimum representation of 50 mm diameter and mosquito-proof protection. Position the vent to avoid nuisance and ensure safe gas dispersion.

Provide properly sized access openings/covers over the compartments for inspection, maintenance and desludging.

Step 10 — Design the tank structure

The hydraulic sizing does not complete the structural design. Separately check:

  • Wall thickness and reinforcement
  • Earth pressure and surcharge
  • Internal water pressure
  • Groundwater uplift / flotation
  • Base slab and bearing condition
  • Cover slab and access-cover loading
  • Watertightness and crack control
  • Durability and concrete exposure
  • Construction joints and pipe penetrations

These checks require site-specific structural parameters and should be designed by the responsible engineer.

Step 11 — Select effluent treatment/disposal

A septic tank is primary treatment; its effluent still needs an appropriate treatment/disposal arrangement. Assess soil permeability/percolation, groundwater, drinking-water sources, buildings, property boundaries and local authority requirements.

Depending on site conditions, options may include a subsurface dispersion/absorption system, dispersion trench, further biological treatment or another approved disposal/reuse system.

Step 12 — Final compliance review

Before construction, verify the complete design against the latest applicable BIS standards, CPHEEO guidance, NBC provisions and local building/sewerage bye-laws. Check that the tank can be accessed for desludging and that no untreated sewage can reach a drain, groundwater source or surface water.

8. Standards, assumptions & engineering notes

Primary standards used
  • IS 2470 (Part 1):1985 — Code of Practice for Installation of Septic Tanks: Design Criteria and Construction. BIS lists the standard as reviewed in 2022.
  • IS 2470 (Part 2):1985 — Secondary Treatment and Disposal of Septic Tank Effluent.
  • CPHEEO Manual on Sewerage and Sewage Treatment Systems — national engineering guidance for sewerage/sanitation planning and design.
  • NBC 2016 / local building bye-laws — check the authority having jurisdiction. Local rules can impose additional separation, chamber, access and disposal requirements.
Key IS 2470 Part 1 values implemented
  • Minimum rectangular tank width: 0.75 m.
  • Minimum liquid depth: 1.0 m; minimum liquid capacity: 1.0 m³.
  • Rectangular length-to-width ratio: 2 to 4.
  • Minimum freeboard: 300 mm.
  • Detention period represented by the process design: 24–48 h.
  • Minimum practical nominal drainage pipe diameter: 100 mm.
  • Ventilation pipe: minimum 50 mm with mosquito-proof mesh.
  • For tanks <1200 mm wide, the outlet dip-pipe is represented as 100 mm nominal bore, with outlet invert 50 mm below inlet invert.
  • Where tank capacity exceeds 2000 L, a two-compartment arrangement is appropriate; the app uses a 2:1 first-to-second compartment split as a practical configuration and flags the need for detailed layout.
IS 2470 Part 1 calculation engine
  • Peak-flow surface area: A = 0.92 × Qp / 10, where Qp is peak flow in L/min.
  • Digested sludge accumulation: 0.00021 m³/person/day (≈76.65 L/person/year).
  • Hydraulic detention volume: Vd = Qavg × detention/24.
  • The app separates hydraulic volume, sludge-storage volume and sedimentation-area checks rather than hiding them in one black-box number.
Important limitations
  • The app is an educational / preliminary design and checking tool. It is not a substitute for the latest licensed copy of the applicable standards, local bye-laws, site investigation or professional design review.
  • The standard household table assumes WC-only discharge; if bath, kitchen and other wastewater are also connected, use measured/approved wastewater flow and verify the treatment/disposal system accordingly.
  • Structural design of RCC/brick walls, cover slab, buoyancy/uplift, soil pressure, seismic effects, groundwater and foundation bearing are not certified by this app. These require site-specific structural design.
  • Never connect septic-tank effluent directly to an open drain or water body without the required treatment/disinfection and regulatory approval.
