Camber selection

The app selects the crossfall from the surface type and rainfall condition. The displayed rate is expressed as a percentage of horizontal width.

Crown rise

For a crowned two-way carriageway, crown rise = (carriageway width / 2) × camber rate. Example: W = 7.0 m and e = 2.0% gives 3.5 × 0.02 = 0.070 m.

Slope interpretation

A camber of e% corresponds to approximately 1 in (100/e). Thus 2% corresponds to 1 in 50.

Formation width

Formation width = carriageway width + 2 × shoulder width. The camber graphic intentionally shows the pavement/cross-section without drawing shoulders, while the width calculation can still include them.

Animation

The moving water/flow representation is a visualization of drainage direction. It does not alter the selected camber calculation.

Calculates reaction lag, braking distance, and dynamic 3-vehicle overtaking zones per IRC:66-1976.

Speed conversion

v = V/3.6, where V is in km/h and v is in m/s.

SSD

The app separates SSD into reaction distance and braking distance: d₁ = 0.278 V t; d₂ = V²/[254(f + G/100)]; therefore SSD = d₁ + d₂.

OSD

For the implemented OSD model, the slower vehicle speed is Vb = max(10, V − 16). The app then calculates the initial following distance, overtaking/manoeuvre distance and opposing-vehicle distance and sums them to obtain OSD.

Animation sequence

A follows B → A moves into the overtaking lane → A overtakes B → A clears B → A returns to B's original lane → A continues in that lane → C continues in its opposing lane → A/C fronts align → loop resets.

Distance telemetry

A–B distance is the longitudinal separation of A and B. A–C front clearance is measured between the two vehicle fronts. The animation speed slider changes only the playback rate; it does not change SSD or OSD.

IRC basis

The OSD animation represents the manoeuvre concept described for two-lane roads in IRC:66-1976. Where required overtaking sight distance cannot be provided, the applicable IRC provisions should be followed for intermediate sight distance and overtaking restrictions.

Horizontal Alignment & Superelevation

IRC 7-step superelevation design, extra widening ($W_e$), and transition curve ($L_s$) analysis (IRC:38-1988).

Superelevation

The app first computes theoretical superelevation from the implemented design-speed/radius relationship and then limits the adopted value to the selected maximum.

Lateral friction

When the adopted superelevation is capped, the remaining demand is represented as lateral friction. The implemented relationship is f = V²/(127R) − e, with V in km/h, R in m and e as decimal.

Extra widening

Mechanical widening is calculated from wheelbase and curve radius: Wmech = nL²/(2R). Psychological widening is added using the app's implemented speed/radius expression, then We = Wmech + Wps.

Transition length

The app evaluates a centrifugal-acceleration-based transition and an empirical IRC transition and adopts the larger value: Ls = max(Ls,C, Ls,IRC).

Banked-road animation

The rotating/banked cross-section is a visualization of the adopted superelevation and centrifugal-force concept. It does not feed pixels or animation state back into the design calculation.

IRC basis

The governing provisions should be read with the applicable IRC geometric-design standard for the road class and terrain. The app's reference list identifies IRC:73 and IRC:86 among its governing references.

Grade difference

The algebraic grade difference is represented by N = |g₁ − g₂|/100. The sign and relative order of g₁ and g₂ determine whether the app identifies a summit or valley curve.

Sight-distance criterion

The app can use SSD, ISD or its OSD criterion. For ISD it uses ISD = 2 × SSD. The selected sight criterion directly changes the required sight distance used by the curve calculation.

SSD used by this module

The implemented expression is SSD = 0.278 V × 2.5 + V²/(254 × 0.35). The resulting value is then used as the selected sight-distance requirement when SSD is chosen.

Curve length

For a summit curve the app uses its implemented parabolic sight-distance relationship; for a valley curve it uses its implemented comfort-based relationship. The displayed length is rounded upward to the next metre.

Animation

The vertical-curve graphic shows the parabolic profile and sightline concept. The moving point is visual only and does not modify the calculated curve length.

IRC basis

Vertical-curve design should be checked against the applicable IRC geometric-design provisions for the road class, including the selected sight-distance criterion and comfort requirements.
