IS 456:2000 Column Design Theory & Technical Manual

1. Structural Behaviour of Compression Members

A reinforced-concrete column is a compression member designed for the factored axial load and the design moments acting about one or both principal axes. IS 456:2000 distinguishes short and slender compression members using the effective-length-to-lateral-dimension ratios.

IS 456 · Cl. 25

Slenderness Classification Cl. 25.1.2

lexD<12  and  leyb<12

Short column: both principal-axis slenderness ratios are less than 12. If either ratio reaches 12, the member is treated as slender for the corresponding design consideration.

Slender column: the additional moment provisions of Cl. 39.7 are considered because lateral deflection produces a second-order P Δ effect.

Design Response

  • Axial compression: the short-column axial capacity is checked using Cl. 39.3 only when its applicability conditions are satisfied.
  • Combined axial load and bending: section resistance is evaluated from strain compatibility with the concrete compression strain limit of 0.0035 used for flexural design.
  • Biaxial bending: Cl. 39.6 combines the two uniaxial moment capacities at the specified axial load.
  • Slenderness: Cl. 39.7 additional moments are included before the final interaction check.

IS 456 Column Design Sequence

Use the following sequence for the design/check implemented by this calculator. The applied actions Pu, Mux and Muy are factored design actions supplied by the user; frame analysis and load-combination generation are outside this calculator.

Step 1: Establish Design Inputs and ActionsInputs / Scope

Step 2: Determine Effective Length and SlendernessCl. 25.1.2, 25.2, Annex E

Determine the effective lengths about the two principal axes and calculate the slenderness ratios. A column is short only when both applicable ratios are below 12; if either ratio reaches 12, the corresponding plane is slender.

lexD<12
leyb<12
l≤60 ×least lateral dimension
l≤100b2D

The first unsupported-length expression applies to the restraint condition used by the calculator; the second is the cantilever limit used in its Cl. 25.3 check. Effective lengths are supplied by the user; the calculator does not independently perform frame stiffness analysis or Annex E end-condition analysis.

Step 3: Check Minimum EccentricityCl. 25.4

Minimum eccentricity is based on the unsupported length l, not the effective length. Check both principal directions.

emin=max⁡(l500+D30,20 mm)

For the other principal direction, use the corresponding lateral dimension b in place of D.

Step 4: Check Cl. 39.3 ApplicabilityCl. 39.3

The simple short axially loaded expression may be used only when the member is short and the minimum eccentricity does not exceed 0.05 times the relevant lateral dimension. Otherwise the combined axial-load/bending or slender-column provisions govern.

emin,x≤0.05D
emin,y≤0.05b

Step 5: Calculate Short Axial CapacityCl. 39.3

For an applicable short axially loaded member, calculate the nominal axial design capacity using the gross concrete area less longitudinal steel area.

Pu=0.4fckAc+0.67fyAsc

This check is reported as N.A. when the Cl. 39.3 applicability conditions are not satisfied.

Step 6: Determine Uniaxial Capacity at the Specified Axial LoadCl. 39.5 / Cl. 39.1–39.2 assumptions

For each principal axis, determine the maximum uniaxial moment capacity corresponding to the specified Pu. The implemented solver uses strain compatibility, equilibrium and the adopted concrete/steel stress relationships.

The resulting capacities are Mux1 and Muy1, i.e. the maximum uniaxial moment capacities for the specified axial load.

Step 7: Calculate Puz and αnCl. 39.6

Calculate the theoretical axial-load reference and determine the exponent used in the biaxial interaction equation.

Puz=0.45fckAc+0.75fyAsc
αn=1 if PuPuz≤0.2

Between 0.2 and 0.8, αn varies linearly from 1.0 to 2.0.

Step 8: Calculate Slender-Column Additional MomentsCl. 39.7 / 39.7.1.1

Where slenderness governs and a separate second-order structural analysis is not supplied, calculate the base additional moment from Cl. 39.7.1 and multiply it by the reduction factor k from Cl. 39.7.1.1.

Max=PuD2000(lexD)2
May=Pub2000(leyb)2
k=Puz−PuPuz−Pb≤1

Pb is the balanced axial load corresponding to concrete compression strain 0.0035 and tensile strain 0.002 in the outermost tension steel layer. The factor k is applied to the Cl. 39.7.1 base moment. For braced columns without transverse loads, the code's initial-moment rule based on end moments must be established by the structural analysis; this calculator uses the supplied governing design moment and does not perform frame analysis.

Step 9: Perform Biaxial Interaction CheckCl. 39.6

Use the maximum uniaxial capacities corresponding to the specified axial load and check the combined interaction.

(MuxMux1)αn+(MuyMuy1)αn≤1.0

For a slender column, the moments entering this check include the applicable additional moments from Step 8.

Step 10: Check Reinforcement, Cover and ConstructabilityCl. 26.4.2.1, 26.5.3

Verify longitudinal steel percentage, minimum number and diameter of bars, peripheral spacing, nominal cover, tie diameter, tie spacing and lateral support/cross-tie arrangement. Where lap splices are used, the longitudinal steel percentage at the splice zone is limited to 4 percent. For circular columns, also verify the minimum number of longitudinal bars and helical requirements where a helix is selected.

0.8%Ag≤Asc≤6%Ag
φtie≥max⁡(φmain,max4,6 mm)
VsVc≥0.36(AgAc−1)fckfy

For a qualifying helix, the strength is taken as 1.05 times that of the corresponding tied column; the calculator permits that enhancement only after its helix checks pass.

Step 11: Final Verification and Governing ResultVerification Log

The final result is based on the applicable axial, bending, slenderness and detailing checks. A condition that is not applicable is reported as N.A., while a failed applicable requirement prevents a passing design status. Review the verification log and modification checklist before adopting the section.

Nominal CoverCl. 26.4.2.1

For a column, nominal cover is not less than 40 mm or the diameter of the longitudinal bar, whichever is greater. For columns with minimum dimension 200 mm or under and longitudinal bars not exceeding 12 mm, 25 mm nominal cover may be used. Fire-resistance cover is a separate requirement under Table 16A.

Longitudinal ReinforcementCl. 26.5.3.1

  • Minimum number of longitudinal bars: 4 for rectangular columns and 6 for circular columns.
  • Spacing of longitudinal bars measured along the periphery: not more than 300 mm.
  • Where bars are lapped, the longitudinal reinforcement at the lap location is limited to 4% of gross area as applicable.
